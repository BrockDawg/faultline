          Real footage

          Two aisle clips from free-licence warehouse video, faces blurred.

          The first real cameras taught the judge something. A camera near the floor made it see a touch that never happened.

          Both structure-contact breaches were false: the frames show the truck crossing side-on in front of the camera, clear of both rack ends. The camera sits near the floor at the end of the aisle, and the floor plane assumed a raised camera.

          The judge now has a gate for this, camera.height. It reads the camera's height above the floor from the four floor marks alone, before any model call, and . Under it, every rule that reads the truck's floor position (separation, spotter, structure contact and speed) is unable to rule, and the ruling gives the height and the line. And the hi-vis rule reads the torso, not the floor, and is still ruled.

          A three-run model read, in the optional words beside a ruling, took a person in a pink shirt for someone with no hi-vis. That changes no verdict, and it shows why a model's words never decide one.

          Protests

          A driver protests a move. A volunteer steward makes the ruling from the evidence pack. The models provide AI steward analysis alongside that decision; their output does not set the verdict. Select a protest to open its evidence pack and race footage.

          The full-recording scan is experimental: it groups sampled visual-change spikes into review windows, and ordinary motion can trigger them. Low-confidence fallback windows stay marked insufficient; they do not establish full-session contact recall. Smoke, cuts, or camera edits can hide an incident. Telemetry is context only and cannot add, remove, or reorder candidates. This page shows stored records; optional SAM masks are evidence only, never a verdict.
