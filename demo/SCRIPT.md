# The three-minute walk

One line per still, spoken the way I would say it to a friend.
Each line names the panel on screen as `(on screen: pNN)`, and `demo/STILLS/` holds one picture per line, named `NN-pNN-<panel>.png`.
Fifteen lines, about three minutes at an easy pace.

1. Every warehouse has cameras, and nobody watches them. When a forklift clips a rack or nearly hits someone, the footage exists, but somebody has to find it and then argue about what the rule says. I run a sim-racing league platform, and my stewards have the same job with race footage. So I built faultline. It finds the moment, measures what happened, and leaves the ruling to a person. (on screen: p01, the title)
2. You give it two things. The rules, written in plain English, the way the warehouse already writes them: wear a vest near the trucks, use a spotter when you reverse, keep your distance from people on foot, don't hit the racks. And the camera footage. (on screen: p02, the rules pack)
3. For every rule, on every clip, it gives one of three answers: broken, fine, or "I can't tell". That last one is the point. It never guesses. When it couldn't measure something, it tells you what it couldn't see. (on screen: p03, the three verdicts)
4. Watch one clip. A man walks along the racks with a forklift working right beside him. Meta's SAM 3.1 finds the truck and the person in every frame. These are the four frames the ruling points at. (on screen: p04, the breach frames)
5. The rule says wear a vest. The colour check finds no vest on him in all 22 frames it could read, and the site's line is 70 percent. So: broken. The number is right there, and under it a hash of the whole record. Remember that. (on screen: p05, the breach ruling)
6. Two Meta models look at the same frames. Muse Wonder writes two sentences about what it sees, and Muse Spark Open reads the rule three times. Their words sit beside the ruling so a person checks it faster. They never make the call. The measurement does. (on screen: p06, the words beside the ruling)
7. Here's the opposite. Two people ahead of the truck, both in vests, every frame. Fine. The colour check settles it, and Wonder is only asked when that check isn't sure. The other rules come back fine too. (on screen: p07, the clear)
8. Now here's why I trust it. A group walks across in front of the truck, but the camera cuts the truck off at the bottom of the picture. You cannot see where it meets the floor. (on screen: p08, the unable frames)
9. So you cannot know if it was moving, and neither can faultline. It says "I can't tell", names that one missing fact, and shows the measurement that left it open. (on screen: p09, the unable ruling)
10. On a real site this runs on a whole shift. Every rule, every five seconds, and a person gets a short list of moments worth a look. My test shift: forty-eight windows, sixteen worth a look, eighty-four fine, a hundred and forty "can't tell", mostly because the camera moved. (on screen: p10, the shift)
11. That hash from earlier? Every ruling writes a record. Turn the network off, delete the footage, run it again, and you get the exact same bytes. Three of those records ship in the repo, so you can check me. (on screen: p11, the replay proof)
12. This is where it started: my own leagues. A driver protests a lap. faultline lines the broadcast up with the car telemetry, cuts the frames at that moment, and lays the facts beside them. The steward still rules. But from evidence, not memory. (on screen: p14, the racing panel)
13. This week I gave it real warehouse footage for the first time: five free clips, one still camera. Three it refused, because the camera moved. On the still one it got something wrong, a touch that never happened, because the camera sat on the floor. Now it refuses a camera that low, and says why. (on screen: p13, the real footage)
14. To do better on a real site it needs three things from the site, not from a model: how high the camera is and what lens it has, the truck's ground clearance, and a second camera on the racks. Some things you can't see from one angle. (on screen: p12, the close)
15. That's faultline. Rules in, rulings out, a number behind every breach, and an honest "I can't tell". For safety leads, insurers, and my own stewards. Thanks for watching. (on screen: p12, the close)
