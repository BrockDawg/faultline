# The three-minute walk

One line per still, spoken the way I would say it to a friend.
Each line names the panel on screen as `(on screen: pNN)`, and `demo/STILLS/` holds one picture per line, named `NN-pNN-<panel>.png`.
Fifteen lines, about three minutes at an easy pace.

1. Hi, I'm Brock. I run PitBot, a sim-racing league platform, and my stewards argue over protest footage every week. So I built faultline: give it a rulebook in plain words and camera footage, and it tells you which rule was broken, shows the frames, and the number that decided it. (on screen: p01, the title)
2. Take a warehouse. Its safety rules go in as written, one per file: hi-vis near the trucks, a spotter when reversing, distance from people on foot, don't hit the racking. (on screen: p02, the rules pack)
3. Every rule gets one of three answers: breach, clear, or "I can't tell". That last one matters. It never guesses. If it couldn't measure something, it says what was missing. (on screen: p03, the three verdicts)
4. Here's a breach. A guy walks along the racking while a truck works next to him. Meta's SAM 3.1 tracks the truck and finds him, and these are the frames the ruling points at. (on screen: p04, the breach frames)
5. And the ruling: no hi-vis on his torso in every frame it could read, against the site's line of seventy percent. Underneath, the record hash. Hold that thought. (on screen: p05, the breach ruling)
6. A model never makes the call. But Muse Wonder looked at the same frames and wrote this, and Muse Spark Open read the rule three times. Those words help a person check faster. They never change the verdict. (on screen: p06, the words beside the ruling)
7. Here's a clear. Two people ahead of the truck, both in vests, every frame. The colour check says so, and Wonder is only asked when it isn't sure. (on screen: p07, the clear)
8. And the honest one. A group crosses in front of the truck, but the frame cuts the truck off at the bottom. You cannot see where it meets the floor. (on screen: p08, the unable frames)
9. So it doesn't guess whether the truck was moving. It says "I can't tell", names the one thing it couldn't measure, and shows the measurement. (on screen: p09, the unable ruling)
10. On a real site this runs on a whole shift: every rule on every window, and a person gets a short list worth a look. My test shift: forty-eight windows, sixteen to review, eighty-four clear, a hundred and forty "can't tell", mostly a moving camera. (on screen: p10, the shift)
11. That record hash? Every ruling rebuilds from its own record, network off, no footage, to the same bytes. Three clips' records ship in the repo, so you can run it yourself. (on screen: p11, the replay proof)
12. My own leagues. A driver files a protest and names a lap. faultline lines the broadcast up with the telemetry, cuts the frames at the steward's moment, and lays the facts beside them. The steward still makes the call, from evidence, not memory. (on screen: p14, the racing panel)
13. This week I tried real warehouse footage: five free clips, one still camera. Three it refused: moving camera. The still one caught it making a mistake, a touch that never happened, because the camera sat on the floor. Now it refuses a camera that low, and says why. (on screen: p13, the real footage)
14. To do better on a real site it needs three things from the site, not a model: the camera's height and lens, the truck's clearance, and a better camera on the racking. Some things you can't see from one angle. (on screen: p12, the close)
15. That's faultline. Rules in, rulings out, a number behind every breach, and an honest "I can't tell". Built for safety leads, insurers, and my own stewards. Thanks for watching. (on screen: p12, the close)
