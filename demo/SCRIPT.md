# The three-minute walk

One line per scene of the film, spoken the way I would say it to a friend.
Each line names the panel on screen as `(on screen: pNN)`, and `demo/STILLS/` holds one picture per line, named `NN-pNN-<panel>.png`.
Fifteen lines, about three minutes at an easy pace.

1. I run PitBot, for sim-racing leagues. When a driver protests a move, a volunteer steward digs through the race footage to rule on it. (on screen: p14, the racing panel)
2. I'm Brock. I built faultline: give it written rules and footage, and it rules on every rule, with the evidence. (on screen: p01, the title)
3. The same judge reads a warehouse, where forklifts hurt people and nobody watches every camera. (on screen: p02, the rules pack)
4. Its rulings serve two people: the safety lead, who gets a short list to act on, and the insurer, who gets evidence instead of a form. (on screen: p12, the close)
5. The site writes its rules in plain English. Meta's open-weight Muse Spark Open, on Baseten, compiles each rule three times; only what all three agree on ships. (on screen: p02, the rules pack)
6. It reads fixed site cameras, the kind every warehouse already has. (on screen: p04, the breach frames)
7. Meta's SAM 3.1 tracks the truck, and finds the people from text alone. Here, a man walks into the truck zone with no hi-vis. (on screen: p04, the breach frames)
8. Four floor points per camera, marked once, give it a measured floor, so scripts measure speed and distance, as bands. (on screen: p05, the breach ruling)
9. A script reads the colour on his torso: breach, with the frames and the number that decided it. Two people ahead of the truck, both vested: clear. (on screen: p07, the clear)
10. Here the frame cuts the truck off, so nobody can see where it meets the floor. It won't guess: unable, naming the one fact it couldn't measure. (on screen: p09, the unable ruling)
11. Then real footage. A camera near the floor made it see a touch that never happened, so now under one and a half metres it won't rule on the floor. (on screen: p13, the real footage)
12. Nobody watches a whole shift. My demo shift: forty-eight windows, sixteen breaches to review, eighty-four clear, a hundred and forty unable — a hundred and ten of them, twenty-two windows times five rules, because the camera moved or was never marked. (on screen: p10, the shift)
13. Back to racing. The steward names the moment; scripts cut the frames, the steward rules from them. Meta's models named the right offender four times in eight. (on screen: p14, the racing panel)
14. On twenty-six graded clips it found twelve of fourteen breach clips, and no false breach on the ten compliant ones. Every ruling rebuilds offline to the same bytes. (on screen: p11, the replay proof)
15. A rules pack is just written rules — the same judge can be taught a crane site, a press, a crossing; those packs are written the same way this one was. Scripts decide, Meta's models add words, a person makes the call. That's faultline. (on screen: p12, the close)
